# The Cattle Ledger — Short #2: "How Much Meat Comes From One Cow?"

**Length:** about 42 s · **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920 · **Voice:** documentary narrator (vidIQ "Brian – Deep, Resonant")

## 1. Final script / voiceover text
> A twelve-hundred-pound cow doesn't give you twelve hundred pounds of meat.
> First, the hide, head, hooves, and organs come off.
> USDA data shows the average beef carcass weighs about sixty-one percent of the live animal.
> So twelve hundred pounds becomes roughly seven hundred thirty-five pounds hanging in the cooler.
> Then come the bones, the fat trim, and the cutting loss.
> USDA figures it takes about two point four pounds of live steer to make one pound of retail beef.
> That leaves around five hundred pounds of beef for your freezer. Less than half the animal.
> Follow The Cattle Ledger for the truth behind the beef business.

**Facts used** (see `facts/beef.md`):
- USDA ERS slaughter weights, Jan–Sep 2026: 1,460 lb live → 894 lb dressed, about 61%.
- ERS price-spread documentation: 2.40 lb of steer per 1 lb of retail beef.
