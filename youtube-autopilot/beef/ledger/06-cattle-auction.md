# The Cattle Ledger — Short #6: "What Happens at a Cattle Auction?"

**Length:** about 48 s · **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920 · **Voice:** documentary narrator (vidIQ "Brian")

## 1. Final script / voiceover text
> This is where cattle prices get real. At a sale barn, cattle walk into the ring, the auctioneer starts the chant, and buyers bid in dollars per hundredweight. That's per hundred pounds.
> Here's what USDA reported for the Oklahoma City feeder cattle market in September 2026.
> Steer calves around five hundred twenty-five pounds brought about three ninety-four a hundredweight. That's just over two thousand dollars a head.
> Heavier steers, around seven hundred seventy-five pounds, brought about three forty-eight a hundredweight, or roughly twenty-seven hundred a head.
> Notice: the lighter calf earns more per pound. Buyers are paying for the weight it can still put on.
> Follow The Cattle Ledger for the truth behind the beef business.

**Facts used:** USDA ERS Livestock prices, Oklahoma City feeder steers M&L #1, September 2026. See `facts/beef.md`.
- 500–550 lb: $394.01/cwt. At 525 lb that's about $2,069 a head.
- 750–800 lb: $347.69/cwt. At 775 lb that's about $2,695 a head.
