# The Cattle Ledger — Short #18: "How Much Beef Do You Get From a Cow? 🥩"

**Live:** https://youtube.com/shorts/_tVv1AWN6Xs

## Script
> A fourteen-hundred-fifty-pound steer gives you only about six hundred pounds of beef at the store. So how much beef do you get from a cow? First comes the carcass: in September, USDA says the average animal dressed out at about eight hundred ninety pounds. Then bone, fat and trim come off. USDA figures it takes about two point four pounds of live animal for every pound of retail beef. At September's Choice beef price of about nine ninety-four a pound, that beef is worth around six thousand dollars at the store. The steer itself sold for about thirty-two hundred. Follow The Cattle Ledger.

**Facts used** (all in facts/beef.md):
- ERS slaughter weights, Sept 2026: 1,451 lb live → 890 lb dressed
- ERS conversion: 2.40 lb of standard steer per lb of retail beef, so 1,451 / 2.4 ≈ 605 lb
- Choice retail value, Sept 2026: 994.4¢, so 605 × $9.944 ≈ $6,016
- Nebraska fed steer $221.67/cwt × 1,451 ≈ $3,216

Cross-check: ERS gross farm value 530.5¢ × 605 lb ≈ $3,210, which matches.
