## 1. Final script / voiceover text
> Cattle aren't cheap to raise. The USDA tracks what it costs to keep one beef cow for a year.
> In 2025, feed alone ran about four hundred thirty-six dollars per cow.
> Add vet bills, fuel, repairs, and interest, and the cash costs hit about six hundred seventy-six.
> But the hidden costs are even bigger: wear on trucks and equipment, about three hundred eighty dollars, and the rancher's own unpaid labor, about five hundred seventy.
> All in, roughly seventeen hundred fifty dollars per cow. Each cow brought in about fourteen hundred seventeen.
> That's a loss of about three hundred thirty-five dollars per cow, even with calf values up almost thirty percent.
> Follow The Cattle Ledger for the truth behind the beef business.

**Facts used:** USDA ERS Commodity Costs and Returns, cow-calf, U.S. total, 2025 ($ per cow). See `facts/beef.md`.
