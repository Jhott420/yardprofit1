# The Cattle Ledger — Short #4: "Why Is Steak So Expensive?"

**Length:** about 50 s · **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920 · **Voice:** documentary narrator (vidIQ "Brian")

## 1. Final script / voiceover text
> There's a reason steak costs so much. In September 2026, a pound of Choice sirloin steak averaged about fourteen dollars in U.S. stores. Ground beef? About six seventy-nine.
> Start with the animal. A fed steer sold for about two dollars and twenty-one cents a pound, live. But it takes about two point four pounds of steer to make one pound of retail beef.
> So before anyone cuts, ships, or sells it, every pound already carries about five dollars and thirty cents of animal.
> Then about forty-four cents of every beef dollar goes to cutting, trucking, and the store.
> And steaks are the premium cuts: twelve sixty-nine a pound on average, versus about seven for ground beef.
> Follow The Cattle Ledger for the truth behind the beef business.

**Facts used** (USDA ERS Meat Price Spreads and BLS retail prices, September 2026; see `facts/beef.md`):
- Sirloin $14.106/lb
- Ground beef $6.792/lb
- All steaks $12.69/lb
- All uncooked ground beef $7.062/lb
- 5-market steer $221.05/cwt
- 2.40 lb of steer per retail lb, so the gross farm value is 530.5¢/lb
- Wholesale-to-retail share 43.5%
